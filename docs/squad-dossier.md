# Call of War — Squad Dossier

## Private John Wilson
**Age:** 21  
**Origin:** Bathurst, New South Wales, Australia  
**Background:** Shearer's son. Quiet, thoughtful, but steady under pressure.

**Personality:**
- Introspective and observant
- Loyal to his squad without being sentimental
- Grows into leadership naturally, not by ambition
- Writes letters to his sister Mary between missions — a way to process his experiences

**Role in the squad:**
- Primary player character
- The person the squad looks to for direction, especially after Smithy's death
- The emotional core — his reactions to events shape the tone

**Story arc:**
- Begins as an inexperienced but determined young soldier
- Hardens through combat and loss
- Takes on responsibility after Smithy's death
- Ends as a changed man, carrying the weight of memory and survival

**Key item:** Smithy's mouth organ (picked up in Mission 3 after Smithy's death)

---

## Tom "Smithy" Smith
**Age:** 23  
**Origin:** Melbourne, Victoria, Australia  
**Background:** Works as a printer's assistant before the war. Quick-witted and irreverent.

**Personality:**
- The squad's morale keeper — always ready with a joke or a witty remark
- Brave but not reckless; his humor masks a sharp mind
- Cares deeply for his mates, especially Wilson
- Plays a battered mouth organ during quiet moments, plays old Australian songs and folk tunes

**Role in the squad:**
- Morale and emotional anchor
- The voice that keeps spirits up during dark times
- Represents the best of the ANZAC spirit: courage, humor, and solidarity

**Story arc:**
- Begins as the squad's cheerful heart
- Becomes more introspective as the campaign progresses and the cost becomes clear
- Dies in Mission 3 (Lone Pine) saving Wilson from enemy grenadier fire
- His death reshapes the entire tone of the campaign

**Death scene:**
During the chaotic tunnel fighting at Lone Pine, Smithy spots an Ottoman soldier about to throw a grenade at Wilson. He pushes Wilson out of the way and takes the blast. He dies in Wilson's arms, still trying to joke ("Well, mate, I reckon that's the end of me song..."). His mouth organ falls to the ground, and Wilson picks it up.

**Legacy:**
- Smithy's mouth organ becomes a symbol of memory and loss
- Wilson carries it for the rest of the campaign
- Other squad members reference Smithy's humor and bravery
- His death is the emotional turning point of the entire game

## Frank O'Brien
**Age:** 34  
**Origin:** Ballarat, Victoria, Australia  
**Background:** Works as a miner before the war. Experienced, tough, and fatherly.

**Personality:**
- The squad's mentor and steady hand
- Older than the others, acts as a father figure
- Gruff but deeply loyal
- Pragmatic and experienced — has seen hardship before the war
- Supports Wilson after Smithy's death, quietly and without fuss

**Role in the squad:**
- Lewis gun/support specialist
- Provides covering fire and tactical advice
- Keeps the group focused and disciplined
- Acts as the bridge between raw recruits and experienced soldiers

**Story arc:**
- Begins as the experienced mentor figure
- Becomes more protective of Wilson after Smithy's death
- Wounded in Mission 4 (Night Attack) but insists on continuing
- Survives the campaign alongside Wilson and Dusty
- Represents the steadfast working-class backbone of the ANZAC force

**Key equipment:** Lewis gun (light machine gun) — becomes iconic to his character

**Relationship with Wilson:**
Frank recognizes Wilson's potential for leadership after Smithy's death. He supports Wilson without making it obvious, allowing Wilson to grow into his role. By the end of the campaign, Frank sees Wilson as his peer.

---

## Billy "Dusty" Rhodes
**Age:** 19  
**Origin:** Queensland, Australia  
**Background:** Bushman and outdoor worker. Natural rifleman and scout.

**Personality:**
- Quiet and observant, speaks rarely but meaningfully
- Natural talent for marksmanship and reconnaissance
- Calm and steady, almost serene in combat
- Deeply affected by Smithy's death, though he shows it quietly
- Represents the Australian connection to the land and survival

**Role in the squad:**
- Scout and marksman
- Handles reconnaissance and sniping
- Provides early warning of enemy positions
- His marksmanship saves the squad multiple times

**Story arc:**
- Begins as a quiet, capable young soldier
- Grows closer to Wilson after Smithy's death
- His quiet strength becomes more evident as the campaign wears on
- Survives the campaign alongside Wilson and Frank
- Represents resilience and quiet determination

**Key equipment:** Rifle (primary marksman weapon), binoculars for scouting

**Relationship with Smithy:**
Dusty and Smithy seem like opposites — one talkative, one quiet — but they share a deep friendship. Smithy's death affects Dusty profoundly, though he expresses it through quiet reflection rather than speech.

## Ottoman Soldiers (Enemies, not villains)

**Important note:** The Ottoman soldiers defending Gallipoli are portrayed with respect and dignity. They are not faceless enemies or villains; they are soldiers defending their homeland against invasion.

**Character:**
- Determined and well-trained defenders
- Fighting for their country and their families
- Under the command of Mustafa Kemal, a brilliant military leader
- Respect the ANZAC soldiers as worthy opponents
- After the war, both sides recognize each other's courage and sacrifice

**Gameplay treatment:**
- Ottoman soldiers react intelligently to player tactics
- Some will retreat, some will hold their ground, some will counterattack
- Voice lines and interactions are in Turkish (with subtitles), emphasizing their humanity
- Occasionally, the player finds personal items on fallen Ottoman soldiers (letters, photographs) that humanize them

## Supporting Characters

### Officers and Non-commissioned officers
**Role:** Provide mission briefings, orders, and tactical guidance  
**Character:** Range from competent to poorly informed (reflecting historical reality)  
**Interaction:** Players take orders from NCOs and junior officers

### Medical staff
**Role:** Appear in missions and cutscenes treating wounded  
**Character:** Dedicated and compassionate, working in terrible conditions  
**Interaction:** Players may escort wounded to medical stations

### Other ANZAC soldiers
**Role:** Squad-mates and supporting units in larger missions  
**Character:** Diverse backgrounds, reflect Australian society  
**Interaction:** Players coordinate with them and may provide supporting fire

## Squad Dynamics & Key Relationships

### Wilson & Smithy
- Smithy is Wilson's best friend and conscience
- Smithy keeps Wilson grounded and reminds him to see the humanity in the chaos
- Smithy's death profoundly changes Wilson

### Wilson & Frank
- Frank is Wilson's mentor and father figure
- Frank recognizes Wilson's potential and quietly guides him
- After Smithy's death, Frank becomes Wilson's emotional anchor

### Wilson & Dusty
- Dusty and Wilson share quiet understanding
- Dusty respects Wilson's growth into leadership
- Together, they survive the campaign, carrying the memory of Smithy

### Smithy & Frank
- Frank appreciates Smithy's humor and resilience
- Smithy teases Frank affectionately but respects his authority
- Frank trusts Smithy with his life

### Smithy & Dusty
- Opposites in temperament but close friends
- Smithy's humor balances Dusty's quietness
- Dusty's steadiness grounds Smithy's impulsiveness

## The Mouth Organ

Smitty's battered mouth organ is more than an instrument — it's a symbol of memory, hope, and the bonds between soldiers.

**Appearances throughout the campaign:**
- **Early missions:** Smithy plays quiet, mournful tunes during quiet moments
- **Mission 3 (Lone Pine):** Smithy dies; Wilson picks up the mouth organ
- **Mission 4 onwards:** Wilson carries the mouth organ. At key moments, he plays it, remembering Smithy
- **Mission 9 (Evacuation Begins):** Wilson plays the mouth organ one last time before leaving Gallipoli
- **Mission 10 ending:** In the epilogue, Wilson plays the mouth organ as the transport ship sails away

**Symbolic weight:**
- Represents Smithy's memory and sacrifice
- Reminds Wilson (and the player) of what was lost
- Symbol of hope and humanity in the midst of war
- Connects the personal story to the larger campaign
